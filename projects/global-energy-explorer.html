04 · group data visualization project

Global Energy Explorer

Type
Group project — poster + report
Tools
Python · Dash (Plotly)
Data
158 countries · 2000–2021, 3,320 records
Team
3 members

Problem / Goal

Build an interactive tool that lets non-experts explore global energy production, CO2 emissions, and economic indicators over 21 years — investigating which energy sources dominate, how they've trended, and whether renewable growth relates to lower emissions.

What I did

Data integration
  • Merged two Kaggle datasets on sustainable energy and global energy mix
  • Handled extensive missing data via imputation, excluding countries with too little usable data
App architecture
  • Built as a multi-page Dash app with a shared navbar and central page container
  • Structured a proper data layer rather than loading data ad hoc per page
  • Added a persistent color-blind-safe mode toggle, stored client-side so the setting survives across sessions
Visualizations
  • World map, bubble charts (GDP vs. renewables vs. emissions), global trend charts, and per-country time series
  • Interaction kept intentionally minimal (filtering + hover) to stay accessible to non-technical users

Key findings

  • Renewable energy growth doesn't consistently translate into proportional CO2 emissions reductions — true for many developed countries, but not universally
  • Developing countries often show high renewable shares out of necessity, but shift toward conventional sources as their economies grow
  • Globally, renewables still make up under 30% of total energy production, while CO2 emissions have risen roughly 50% since 2000
