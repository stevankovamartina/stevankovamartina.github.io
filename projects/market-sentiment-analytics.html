News & Market Sentiment Analytics

Type
Individual exam project
Tools
Python · scikit-learn · VADER · FinBERT
Dataset
~19,000 headlines · S&P 500 (2008–2024)
Method
TF-IDF, lexicon & transformer sentiment

Problem / Goal

Test whether short-term S&P 500 market direction (up/down next day) can be predicted from financial news headlines, comparing text-based and sentiment-based modeling approaches.

What I did

Data preprocessing
  • Cleaned and parsed the raw CSV; verified no missing values across 19,127 observations
  • Aggregated same-day headlines into daily documents (~9,000 daily observations), aligning text with daily market outcomes
  • Used a time-consistent train/test split (train on older data, test on more recent) to respect time order and avoid leakage
Model 1 — TF-IDF baseline
  • TF-IDF vectorization of headlines feeding a logistic regression classifier
  • Included dummy classifiers as sanity-check baselines
Model 2 — Lexicon & transformer sentiment
  • VADER: converted headlines to sentiment scores, aggregated to daily features (mean, std dev, share positive/negative)
  • FinBERT: applied a financial-domain transformer per headline, built a sentiment score from positive/negative probabilities
  • Trained logistic regression on both sets of daily sentiment features, using consistent ROC-AUC evaluation throughout

Key findings

  • TF-IDF baseline performed slightly below random guessing (accuracy ≈0.49, ROC-AUC ≈0.46)
  • VADER and FinBERT both modestly outperformed the baseline (ROC-AUC ≈0.52–0.523), but remained close to random
  • FinBERT's financial-domain training gave only a marginal edge over the simpler VADER lexicon
  • Conclusion: news headlines alone are not sufficient to reliably predict short-term market direction — the value of the project is in the rigorous comparison of approaches and honest reporting of a negative result, not in producing a working trading signal

Skills demonstrated

Text preprocessing TF-IDF vectorization Lexicon sentiment (VADER) Transformer sentiment (FinBERT) Time-aware train/test splitting Model evaluation (ROC-AUC) Honest reporting of negative results
